How Auditarr works

Every night it looks at every file in your Plex library through Sonarr, Radarr and Bazarr, finds what will not play right, fixes what is safe to fix, and tells you about the rest in plain words. Nothing is ever deleted; anything it moves can be put back with one click.

The nightly loop

What a finding is

One file (or one missing episode), one problem, one sentence that says what is wrong and what fixes it. The coloured word says how bad:

won't playwrongwaitingFYI
Won't playNo audio or no video, a zero-byte or corrupt file, a sample instead of the episode.
WrongFrench audio on an English show, a Blu-ray tag on a 720p file, an episode that is really a different episode.
WaitingNo subtitles yet while Bazarr searches, a missing episode the indexers have not found, a duplicate copy.
FYI10-bit, AV1 or Dolby Vision that one of your players cannot play directly; an extended cut; a harmless title mismatch.

Every button is reversible

What it does on its own

Where it runs

One containerDocker on Unraid or compose. Needs the arr addresses and keys (Setup page), and the media folder mounted at the same path the arrs use.
Its own databaseSQLite in the config folder: findings, actions, history, settings. Backed up nightly; a weekly drill boots the backup to prove it restores.
Tested like it mattersSynthetic media with known defects, a fake arr stack, a browser bot clicking the real buttons, chaos and slow-service drills, all in CI.
Safe by constructionA read-only mode for staging, a write ledger, an import hook with its own token, optional sign-in with a read-only account for the family.
